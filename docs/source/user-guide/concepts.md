# Concepts

Background on *why* the app behaves the way it does — not required reading
to use it, but useful if something surprises you.

## How the app picks a recommended analysis

The three ranked options on the Analysis Selection screen come from your
Q2–Q6 answers, run through a fixed decision order (roughly, in priority):

1. Comparing more than two periods, without a time trend → **Descriptive
   Summary**.
2. Comparing more than two periods, with a time trend → **Run Chart**.
3. Not comparing anything, but comparing groups → **Descriptive Summary**.
4. Tracking a percentage/proportion over time with 12+ points → **p-Chart**.
5. Tracking a rate or count over time with 12+ points → **u/c-Chart**.
6. Tracking anything over time with fewer than 12 points → **Run Chart**.
7. Comparing an average/median before vs. after → **Before/After: Mean**.
8. Comparing a percentage before vs. after → **Before/After: Proportion**.
9. Anything else → **Run Chart** as a safe default.

The other two options shown alongside the recommendation are simply the
remaining analyses in that same order — so you always see the top pick plus
two reasonable alternatives, never a random assortment. You are never
restricted to the recommendation; picking a different one just changes which
columns you're asked to map next.

## What happens to your data

- Your uploaded CSV/Excel file is encrypted at rest the moment it's
  received, and stored outside the database.
- Full spreadsheet rows are **never** sent to any AI provider. AI features
  (intake pre-fill, plain-language interpretation) only ever see your typed
  project description, your own chat messages, and small pre-computed
  summaries like `result_summary` — never raw row-level data.
- Anything that *is* sent to AI (your description, your chat messages) is
  scrubbed first: names, dates, medical record numbers, phone numbers,
  emails, Social Security numbers, and street addresses are detected with
  both hand-written patterns and a clinical-language AI model, then replaced
  with `[REDACTED]` before the request leaves the server. When a redaction
  happens, you'll see a banner telling you so — it's not a silent
  substitution.
- The app is defense-in-depth, not a guarantee: still avoid typing patient
  names, MRNs, or other identifiers into free-text fields in the first
  place. The scrubber catches accidents; it isn't a reason to test it on
  purpose.

## The mentor review model

A mentor share link is deliberately **view-only**. A mentor can open the
link in any browser without an account, see the same report a resident
would download, and leave comments — but they can never download the Word
or PDF file, and they can never edit your report content. This is a firm
product boundary, not a missing feature: reports are meant to reflect the
resident's own analysis and wording, reviewed and commented on, not
rewritten by someone else.

Share links expire automatically after 30 days but can be revoked or
regenerated by the resident at any time (see {doc}`how-to`). Revoking a link
immediately locks the mentor out — there's no grace period.
